Dual currency deposit / investment, gold-linked (DCD / DCI / "premium" account)
Tenor 1 week to 3 months (Citi HK goes to 6 months); minimums USD10,000 (Citi HK), SGD30,000 (StanChart gold PCI), MYR40,000-50,000 (CIMB / HSBC Malaysia); strike set 1-5% out of the money for the short tenors; indicative annualised coupons typically mid-single-digit to low-teens depending on gold volatility and strike distance - Citi's own worked examples use 10.0% p.a. and 9.7% p.a. on a 1-month ticket, and CIMB advertises "potential double-digit returns p.a.". No early termination right for the client on most versions.
The client places a deposit in a base choice (USD, AUD, EUR, SGD, MYR...) or in gold, picks an alternate choice and a strike (target conversion rate), and a tenor of typically 1 week to 3 months. Economically the client sells the bank a put option on the alternate choice with the same strike and expiry; the option premium is paid out as the "enhanced interest", so the all-in coupon is deposit rate + annualised premium. At the notification/fixing date the bank compares gold-per-base against the strike: if the alternate choice has weakened through the strike the bank exercises and repays principal plus interest converted into the alternate choice at the strike; otherwise it repays in the base choice. Interest normally accrues on an ACT/360 or 30/360 basis and is paid in whichever asset is delivered, so a gold-converted ticket pays interest in ounces.
What the client takes on. This is not a deposit: it is an unsecured claim on the bank plus a short option, so if gold falls through the strike the client is long gold bought at the strike and the coupon is only a partial buffer - Citi's sheet warns of a maximum loss of 100% of the investment amount. The client also gives up all upside beyond the coupon and cannot break the trade.
Citibank (Hong Kong) Limited / Citibank N.A. Desk supplies. The bullion desk sells the wealth business the gold volatility (short-dated 1w-3m ATM/out of the money puts and calls), the gold against the dollar and gold/cross forward points to price the strike, and the unallocated metal account that receives the ounces on conversion. Every converted ticket creates a loco-London unallocated balance that the desk can then bid for as custody or roll into a deposit - the DCI book is a metal-liability feeder for the desk.
Lombard / margin lending against precious metal
Advance rates published by banks are rare; observed ranges are roughly 50-70% on physical bullion and unallocated metal at conventional lenders, up to 80% at the aggressive end (Emirates Money's DMCC-warranted gold loan advertised up to 80% LTV, minimum AED30,000, 6-36 months), 70-75% on major gold ETFs (Saxo gives 75% collateral value to Rating-1 stocks/ETFs), and materially less - often 0-50% - on single-issuer structured notes and gold-mining equities. Non-bank specialists quote 70-85% LTV at 5.5-9.9% interest for 6-24 months. Retail-regulated markets cap it: RBI caps Indian gold loans at 75% LTV.
The client pledges allocated bars in the bank's vault, an unallocated metal account, a metal exchange-traded fund or gold-linked notes; the bank assigns each position a lending value (market value x advance rate) and lends up to the aggregate lending value, usually as a multi-currency revolving facility or overdraft priced off SOFR/SONIA/SARON plus a spread. The collateral is marked daily; if exposure exceeds the lending value the client gets a margin call (top up cash/metal, repay, or reduce), and after a short cure period the bank can sell the metal or close the position itself under the pledge. Allocated bars in the bank's own vault get the best treatment because the bank has possession; third-party-vaulted or ETF collateral gets a haircut for transfer and liquidation risk.
What the client takes on. It is a leveraged long gold position in the form of a loan: at 70% loan-to-value a 25-30% drawdown triggers a margin call, and collateral can be sold to meet it, which realises the position and the tax event the borrowing was arranged to defer. Cross-collateralisation clauses mean a shortfall elsewhere in the portfolio can draw on the metal, and with unallocated collateral the client ranks as an unsecured creditor of the bank.
Emirates NBD (and Emirates Money) Gold-backed financing: gold loans, leasing and precious-metals repo against vaulted bullion / gold certificates; Saxo Bank Collateral value on stock/ETF positions (incl. gold ETFs/ETCs) for margin trading; Standard Chartered Private Bank / HSBC Global Private Banking / UBS / Julius Baer / EFG / Quintet Lombard lending against gold ETFs, gold funds and metal account balances
Desk supplies. The desk provides the valuation source (LBMA PM price or intraday gold mid) used to mark the collateral, the liquidation bid that makes the pledge enforceable (the bank must know it can sell 400oz bars or unallocated same-day), the vault/allocation records behind the pledge, and a hedge if the bank wants to cap its own market risk. A bullion desk that will commit a firm liquidation bid lets the credit committee raise the advance rate - that is the desk's direct revenue lever into the wealth book.
Selling puts on gold to enhance yield (cash-secured puts / gold-linked notes)
Tenor 2 weeks to 3 months (1 month is the standard ticket); strike 3-10% below spot; notional 100 oz to several thousand oz - DBS quotes paper gold in a 100 oz minimum, and its worked example is 1,000 oz; indicative annualised yields 6-15% depending on volatility and strike, quoted on a 360-day basis. Assignment is cash-settled into an unallocated metal account rather than bars unless the client asks for allocation.
The client sells a European put on gold against the dollar (or on GLD/IAU for a US-domiciled account) struck 3-10% below spot, with cash or a credit line set aside to fund delivery, and receives the premium up front. In private banking the trade is usually wrapped as a note sold at a discount to par - DBS Hong Kong's Gold-Linked Note is exactly this: the investor buys a note on paper gold at a discounted price and receives par in cash at maturity if gold fixes at or above strike, or takes delivery of the metal at strike if it fixes below. Adding an American up-and-out knock-out above spot turns it into a KO Gold-Linked Note: the note terminates early with the accrued yield if gold rallies through the barrier, which is how the desk pays a higher headline coupon.
What the client takes on. A standard unleveraged short put has substantial but bounded downside: the worst case is the metal falling to zero, so the maximum loss is the strike less the premium received, per ounce sold. In practice a 20% sell-off leaves the client owning metal well below their entry with only the premium as compensation. The position is usually illiquid: most private-bank notes carry no early termination right and any unwind is at the issuer's own mark. Leveraged, accumulating or knock-out variants behave differently and can lose the full amount invested, so the specific product's leverage and margin terms decide the real exposure.
DBS Bank (Hong Kong) Gold-Linked Note (GLN) and Knock-Out Gold-Linked Note (KO GLN) on Paper Gold; Citibank (Hong Kong) Gold Premium Investment; Standard Chartered Private Bank, HSBC Global Private Banking Asia, J.P. Morgan PB, UBS GWM OTC XAU options / fixed-coupon and yield-enhancement notes on gold
Desk supplies. Short-dated gold volatility, the delta hedge, and the unallocated metal account that receives assigned ounces. The desk also sets the strike grid the RMs quote from (spot minus 3/5/7/10% at 1w/1m/3m) and should publish an indicative yield board daily - that board is what makes the product sellable at scale.
Covered calls on gold, and packaged gold covered-call funds
Bilateral: 1-3 month tenor, strike 3-8% above spot, premium 1-3% of notional for a 1-month at 5% out of the money in normal volatility - annualising to roughly 8-20% depending on the volatility regime. Funds: monthly distributions, headline yields around 8-11% in the 2026 gold volatility environment, MER 0.7-1.0%, partial overwriting (typically 25-50% of the book) to preserve some upside.
Against an existing holding - allocated bars, an unallocated account or a gold ETF - the client sells a call struck above spot and keeps the premium. If gold fixes above the strike the metal is called away at the strike (or cash-settled against the account), so the client has converted uncapped upside into a defined coupon. The packaged version does the same systematically inside a fund: hold gold ETFs (or gold-producer equities), write short-dated calls on a portion of the book, and distribute the premium monthly. Some funds use a synthetic version - long call options on GLD instead of GLD itself, with short calls written against them - which changes the risk profile materially.
What the client takes on. If gold rallies through the strike the metal is called away and the position participates only up to that level, which is what holders noticed in 2025-26 when gold moved several hundred dollars in a matter of weeks. The premium cushions a limited amount of downside. On packaged funds, part of the distribution can be return of capital, so the headline yield and the economic return are not the same number.
Standard Chartered (VCC platform) with Allianz Global Investors as sub-manager Signature Select Enhanced Gold Income Fund; First Trust / Vest (US listed) FT Vest Gold Strategy Target Income ETF (IGLD); Global X Investments Canada Global X Gold Producer Equity Covered Call ETF (GLCC, TSX)
Desk supplies. The desk supplies the call volatility and, critically, the delivery mechanics: if the metal is allocated, someone has to be able to deliver bars or unallocated against assignment. For the fund format the desk can be the options counterparty or the FX/metal hedge provider on the currency-hedged share classes (which is where the 30bp-a-year hedged-share-class cost sits - see our ETF calculations work).
Gold accumulators and decumulators (AQ / DQ)
Tenor 3-12 months, daily fixings, strike 2-6% below spot for gold, knock-out 2-5% above initial spot, 2x gearing below strike, margin c.40% of notional, minimum notionals typically USD1m+ and professional-investor only. Decumulator terms mirror this.
An accumulator is a series of daily (or weekly) forward purchases of a fixed quantity of metal at a strike set at a discount to spot - historically 10-20% for equities, tighter (typically 2-6%) for gold because metal volatility is lower. Two features define the payoff: an up-and-out knock-out, so the contract terminates as soon as spot closes above a barrier a few percent over the initial fixing (capping the client's profit at a few days or weeks of accrued discount); and a gearing/multiplier, so on any fixing below the strike the client must take two (sometimes four) times the daily quantity. A decumulator mirrors it: the client sells metal daily at a premium, with the doubling obligation on the upside - which is the version that has no natural cap on loss. Tenor is usually 3-12 months and the client posts margin, often around 40% of the full notional, against mark-to-market.
What the client takes on. The payoff is asymmetric: the upside knocks out after a modest gain while the downside continues at a doubled quantity for the full tenor. Hong Kong Monetary Authority guidance notes that accumulator investors can be exposed up to the full notional, and that for decumulators the exposure is open-ended if the underlying rallies. In a fast sell-off the client keeps accumulating twice the daily quantity, and any Lombard line behind it faces margin calls at the same time.
HKMA / SFC (regulatory frame, Hong Kong) Guidance on Selling of Accumulators (14 March 2025; predecessor circulars 2010 and 2014); Asian private banks generally (HSBC GPB Asia, Standard Chartered PB, UBS, JPM PB, DBS PB) OTC accumulators / decumulators on XAU; DBS Bank (Hong Kong) OTC Accumulator & Decumulator product booklets (equity; FX incl. target and pivot-target variants)
Desk supplies. The desk prices and hedges the strip - a long stream of short puts with a barrier, so it needs a barrier-capable gold volatility book and daily fixing capacity - and supplies the daily fixings, the metal delivery into the client's unallocated account, and the risk transfer to the interbank market. If the wealth business wants gold AQ/DQ, the desk has to be able to warehouse skew and barrier risk; that is the gating capability.
Gold-linked structured notes
Tenors 6 months to 3 years (shark-fin and structured deposits often 3-12 months); minimums USD100k-250k at PB level, USD10k-50k for the retail structured-deposit versions; issuer is the bank or a G-SIB dealer; underlying is gold against the dollar spot fix or a gold ETF (GLD/IAU) for US-facing paper. Notes on gold have been issued in volume in 2026 - e.g. auto-callable notes linked to SPDR Gold Shares priced in June 2026 with annual call observations to 2029.
The issuer takes the client's principal, keeps a zero-coupon bond's worth to repay whatever capital is protected, and spends the rest on an option package on gold or a gold ETF; the note's terms are the option package. Notes are issued off an EMTN programme or as a structured deposit, with an issuer credit risk the client cannot diversify. In Asia the same economics appear as structured deposits (onshore China, Turkey) for regulatory reasons. Almost all PB notes are illiquid: the issuer quotes a bid, there is no exchange, and early exit costs 1-5 points.
What the client takes on. Issuer credit risk plus gap risk at the barrier plus illiquidity: the client's exit is the issuer's own mark, protection is only at maturity, and in the high-coupon formats (RC/FCN/autocall) the client is short a put that pays out exactly when gold falls hard and everything else in the portfolio is also falling.
DBS Bank (Singapore), Treasures Private Client Gold Linked Notes (GLNs); Major US dealers (e.g. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Citigroup) via SEC-registered 424(b)(2) pricing supplements Auto-callable notes linked to SPDR Gold Shares; HSBC China; Hang Seng Bank; Bank of East Asia Gold-linked structured deposits / capital-protected structured notes
Desk supplies. Vanilla and exotic gold volatility (barriers, digitals, cliquets), the forward curve and lease rates that set the zero-coupon/forward pricing, and the ability to warehouse or recycle the resulting skew and barrier risk. The desk should also quote the secondary bid - a wealth business cannot scale a note shelf without a reliable unwind price.
Gold leasing / metal deposits that pay a yield in metal
Tenors 1, 3, 6 and 12 months are the market convention; minimums from 50-100g (Turkish participation banks) to 1kg+ or 100oz+ for bank-level deposits; rates track the lease-rate curve - historical gold deposit rate averages were c.1.4% (1989-99), 0.54% (2000-09) and 0.24% (2010-19), with spikes (2.77% in October 2008) and again during the 2025-26 tightness. Retail 'gold grows gold' products quote a fixed grams-uplift instead (e.g. 2% for 12 months).
The client transfers title (or grants use) of metal to the bank for a fixed term; the bank becomes a debtor in ounces and pays a deposit rate. The bank funds itself in metal: it can on-lend to a refiner, miner or fabricator who needs metal working capital, use the metal in its own consignment/leasing book, or swap it for dollars (sell spot, buy forward) and capture the difference between the dollar rate it earns and the lease rate it pays. That is where the yield genuinely comes from - the gold lease rate is classically defined as the dollar rate minus the gold forward rate (LIBOR - GOFO), so a metal deposit is really a collateralised dollar deposit expressed in ounces. Interest is paid in metal (extra grams) or in currency, and the client gets back different bars from the ones deposited - the key disclosure point.
What the client takes on. The client is an unsecured creditor in ounces: if the bank fails, the claim ranks with other creditors and the specific bars are gone (the metal is fungible and has been used). There is no deposit insurance on metal in most jurisdictions, early break is usually penal, and any bar-return obligation is 'same weight and fineness', not 'same bars'.

The desk sets the client deposit rate off its own lease-rate curve, manages the borrower side (refiners, jewellers, catalyst users), and owns the credit decision on who the metal goes to. Client gold forwards and prepaid forwards
Tenors 1-12 months standard (longer needs exceptional approval at most banks); minimum 100 oz gold / 10,000 oz silver at DBS-type shelves; margined forwards require 5-20% initial margin with daily marks; prepaid forwards typically advance 70-90% of spot value.
A forward locks a price for delivery in 1-12 months; the forward price is spot plus the dollar rate minus the lease rate, so in a normal market the client pays a small premium to defer, and in a squeeze (backwardation, as in parts of 2025-26) the deferral can be free or better. A prepaid forward reverses the cash flow: the client sells metal forward and takes most of the cash today, with the bank holding the metal or the delivery obligation - economically a secured loan repaid in metal, often more tax- and balance-sheet-friendly than a Lombard loan. Private banks offer forwards on a margined basis (e.g. DBS HK: buy/sell paper gold forward, covered or margined, up to 1-year tenor) so the client can defer payment while fixing the price.
What the client takes on. Full linear price risk with leverage: a margined forward gets margin-called like any leveraged position and the bank can close it out. On a prepaid forward the client has irreversibly sold upside above the forward price and carries the bank's performance risk on the metal return.
DBS Bank (Hong Kong) Forward transactions on Paper Gold and Silver (covered and margined); Bank Julius Baer, Pictet Wealth Management, LGT, Bank of China (HK), Hang Seng Precious-metals spot/forward/option and margin trading for private clients
Desk supplies. Forward points, lease-rate curve, margin methodology and the loco-London delivery leg. The desk's forward curve is the raw material for every other structure on this list - DCI strikes, note zeros and accumulator strips all price off it.
Physical gold repo / sale-and-repurchase for wealthy clients
Tenors overnight to 12 months (3-6 months typical); haircuts 5-20% depending on bar form and location; documentation under a bespoke sale-and-repurchase agreement or GMRA-style terms; metal must be LBMA Good Delivery or refinery-warranted for the best pricing.
Title in the bars passes to the bank against cash at (or slightly below) spot; simultaneously the client agrees to repurchase the same weight and fineness at a forward date and price. The repurchase price minus the sale price is the financing cost, expressed as a repo rate; the haircut (sale price versus market) is the bank's margin protection and is re-marked periodically like a securities repo. Because the bank owns the metal outright for the term, it can lease it, use it to square a short or deliver it into its own clearing obligations - which is why a repo is usually cheaper for the client than a Lombard loan against the same bars. Variable-margin versions add daily marks and margin transfers, just like GMRA.
What the client takes on. Legal title passes on the first leg, so between the two dates the client holds a contractual claim rather than ownership of the metal, and ranks as an unsecured creditor of the bank. Mark-to-market calls can require cash top-ups, and if the repurchase does not take place the client's economics are settled at the repo price.
Emirates NBD Precious Metals REPO alongside gold loans and leasing; Bullion banks / ICBC Standard, and our own book Metal and commodity repo with corporates and holders
Desk supplies. Directly the desk's balance sheet: the desk provides the cash, takes the metal, sets the haircut and lends the metal on. This is the highest-margin way for a bullion desk to monetise a wealth relationship, and it is the private-client analogue of the doré registered-pledge and refining-catalyst REPO deal shapes already on our live structure list.
Precious-metal options and collars for private clients
Tenors 1-12 months most commonly; minimum notionals roughly USD250k-1m equivalent (2,000-20,000 oz is institutional; private clients trade 100-5,000 oz); collar strikes commonly -10% put / +12-15% call for 6-12 months depending on skew; margin required against the short leg.
The PB desk quotes the client vanilla gold against the dollar options (or gold against EUR, CHF, JPY, AUD), risk reversals and zero-cost collars - buy a protective put, sell a call to pay for it - as well as leveraged forwards (target redemption, pivot) for clients who want a better-than-forward rate in exchange for gearing. Options are booked bilaterally under an ISDA or the bank's own derivatives terms, margined against the Lombard line, and can be unwound before expiry at the desk's price. For a client with a very large physical position, a collar is the cleanest way to protect a concentrated holding without selling it and without a tax event.
What the client takes on. Short option legs can be margin-called, and a collar caps participation on an asset the client may be holding for protection. Leveraged forwards such as target-redemption structures and pivots behave differently from a plain forward: they knock out in the client's favourable scenario and gear up in the unfavourable one, in the same way an accumulator does.
Bank Julius Baer Leveraged precious-metals trading and OTC derivatives (24-hour FX & precious metals desk); Citi Private Bank Hedging and OTC derivatives on specific positions (Capital Markets); Hang Seng Bank Gold Master - FX and precious-metal margin trading (XAU with leverage)
Desk supplies. gold against the dollar and gold-cross volatility surfaces, skew, and a quoting service the RMs can hit in size; plus margin and collateral integration with the Lombard book. The desk should publish a daily indicative collar grid - it is the easiest cross-sell into any client holding physical.
Gold notes, funds and ETFs as Lombard collateral (the leverage loop)
Advance rates of roughly 70-75% for major physical gold ETFs, 40-70% for capital-protected notes, 0-40% for reverse convertibles/autocallables, 0% for anything with a knock-in already breached; all revisable at the bank's discretion at any time.
Once a client owns a gold-linked note, fund or ETF, it enters the Lombard collateral schedule with its own advance rate: liquid physical gold ETFs typically get a high lending value (Saxo publishes 75% for Rating-1 ETFs), capital-protected notes issued by the lending bank itself get a moderate one because the payoff floor is known, and leveraged or barrier notes get little or none. The client then borrows against the position and reinvests, which is how a 5% note coupon becomes a 9% levered coupon on the pitch page. Self-issued paper creates a wrong-way problem - the collateral and the borrower's credit exposure are the same bank - so banks usually haircut their own notes harder or exclude them.
What the client takes on. Compounding: when gold falls, the note's mark falls faster than spot (short-put gamma), the lending value is cut at the same moment, and the margin call lands while the client cannot exit the note except at the issuer's bid. This is the classic path from 'conservative income product' to forced liquidation.
HSBC Global Private Banking Asia Lombard lending including against ETFs and structured notes; Saxo Bank Published collateral-value schedule for ETFs
Desk supplies. Nothing directly, but the desk should care: leverage on gold notes is what converts a modest wealth allocation into large delta and vega that the desk has to warehouse, and it is the transmission mechanism for forced selling in a gold drawdown.
Shariah-compliant gold products (bai' as-sarf, murabahah, wakalah, participation accounts)
Entry points are deliberately tiny in retail-Islamic markets - MIGA-i starts at RM10 - while participation deposits run 1-12 months at 50-100g minimums; returns are quoted as profit-share ratios or expected rates rather than interest.
Gold is a ribawi commodity, so a compliant trade must be spot with constructive possession on both legs (bai' as-sarf); deferred settlement, margin and most conventional options are out. The compliant shelf therefore looks like: (a) gold investment accounts backed 100% by allocated metal under wakalah (the bank is the client's agent) or murabahah (cost-plus sale with immediate title transfer); (b) gold instalment purchase - murabahah where the client takes title now and pays over time; (c) participation/profit-share gold deposits, where the metal is deployed in the real economy and the return is a share of actual profit, paid in gold; (d) Shariah dual-currency-style structures built from wa'd (unilateral promise) and murabahah legs rather than options. AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 57 on Gold (issued 2016 with the World Gold Council) is the reference text and unlocked most of this product set.
What the client takes on. Same market risk as any gold holding, plus documentation risk (whether the client truly has title to allocated metal or merely a contractual claim) and the fact that profit-share returns are not guaranteed. Compliance opinions differ by jurisdiction - a structure approved in Malaysia may not pass in the Gulf.
AAOIFI with the World Gold Council Shari'ah Standard No. 57 on Gold and its Trading Controls (2016); Maybank Islamic (Malaysia) Maybank Islamic Gold Account-i (MIGA-i); Bank Islam Malaysia Bank Islam Gold Account (BIGA-i)
Desk supplies. Physical supply into compliant allocated accounts (kilobars, 995/9999 to local standards), same-day delivery-versus-payment so possession is real, and a compliant financing structure (murabahah/tawarruq on metal) if the wealth business wants a Shariah Lombard equivalent. Note that the desk's conventional lease/repo mechanics generally do NOT translate - a compliant metal financing has to be built from sale contracts, not loans.
Gold accumulation / savings plans (GAP) - and why they are NOT accumulators
Minimums from RM10 / RMB100 / JPY1,000 per month; spreads of roughly 0.3-1.0% per side; physical conversion at 10g/50g/100g/1kg denominations plus a fabrication fee; no maturity.
The client commits a fixed cash amount per month (or per day, in the Japanese and Chinese formats); the bank buys the equivalent weight of unallocated gold at its own price, accrues the grams in a metal passbook account, and allows conversion to physical bars or coins above a threshold weight, usually for a fabrication fee. There is no option, no leverage and no knock-out. The bank's margin is the bid/offer on each purchase (CMB, for example, widened its spot gold account spread from RMB2.4 to RMB3.0 per gram in February 2026) plus any storage or conversion fee.
What the client takes on. Unallocated means unsecured: the client is a creditor of the bank for the grams. The spread is the real cost and compounds with frequent purchases, and the conversion fee can be several percent on small bars.
China Merchants Bank, ICBC (积存金) and Chinese peers Gold accumulation plan / 黄金账户; Maybank Islamic, Bank Islam (Malaysia); Pegadaian, BSI (Indonesia) Gold investment accounts / cicil emas instalment gold
Desk supplies. Aggregated client accumulation flow is a daily physical/unallocated hedging requirement and a stable metal liability the desk can fund from - this is the flow a bullion desk wants to win when it pitches a wealth business, even though the product itself looks unglamorous.
Private participation in streaming, royalty and refinery/producer financings
Minimum tickets USD1-5m; 3-10 year lock-ups; target IRRs 8-15%; a feeder-fund or note wrapper with annual or semi-annual distributions; professional/accredited investors only.
The bank syndicates a slice of a metal-linked private transaction to a small group of qualifying clients, usually through a feeder fund, a note issued off the bank's platform, or a direct participation. The underlying is a gold or silver stream (upfront cash for the right to buy future production at a fixed low price), a net smelter royalty, a producer prepay, or secured working capital to a refiner or trader. Returns come as contracted metal deliveries or fixed coupons secured on inventory and receivables, usually in the high single to low double digits, with tenors of 3-10 years and very limited liquidity.
What the client takes on. Single-asset, single-jurisdiction operating risk (a stream is only worth what the mine produces), deep illiquidity, and a valuation the client cannot independently verify. These are the structures where a gold-price rally does not save a bad orebody.
Elliott / JPMorgan / Goldman Sachs (template transaction) c.US$300m margin loan on Triple Flag shares plus an equity forward; Private banks generally (UBS, JPM PB, Citi PB) via private-markets platforms Feeder access to metals-linked private credit and royalty funds
Desk supplies. The desk supplies the hedge (forward sales or options fixing the metal price on the stream/prepay), the offtake and physical logistics, and the price marks used to value the position. Nothing else on the wealth shelf ties as directly into the desk's structured-financing archetypes.

A potential shelf↑

Three tiers. Each maps to a competitor product that already exists.

  • Tier 1 (private bank, all booking centres): an allocated precious-metals account in the group's own London vault with physical delivery via a secure-logistics partner, plus Lombard lending against it. Template: JPMorgan PB vault claims; Julius Baer / LGT metal accounts.
  • Tier 2 (affluent and private-client tiers in SG, HK, UAE, UK): an unallocated gram-gold account with physical redemption in kilobars or 100 g, priced off loco-London and desk-hedged. Template: UOB Gold Savings, Emirates NBD gram gold, HSBC Wayfoong Statement Gold; an existing paper-gold product could be extended beyond one booking centre and given a delivery right.
  • Tier 3 (yield and structured): two income sources that must be kept apart. A gold dual-currency investment, in which the client sells an option and the desk prices and hedges it, so the enhanced return is option premium. Separately, a gold deposit paying a return in metal, where the return comes from lending the metal on at the lease rate; a standing gold autocall/reverse-convertible programme; a house strategic-allocation view implemented via a physically backed vehicle. Templates: CMB, Kuveyt Türk, CIMB Gold Convertible, HSBC China structured deposits, ANZ Private gold fund.
  • KPIs to set now: metal-account balances and account count by booking centre, physical delivered (kg), Lombard drawn against metal, structured-note issuance on XAU, share of PB clients holding any metal product.
