2 minHSBC, Standard Chartered and ANZ head-to-head

The three regional banks the wealth business meets most often in Asia, from dedicated passes over every country site, product brochure and annual report.

  • HSBC (US$2.1trn wealth balances, over US$1trn in Asia): the client gold shelf is deep in Hong Kong and thin everywhere else. Hong Kong has Wayfoong Statement Gold (1 mace units, spread capped at 4%, no delivery, available to Global Private Banking accounts) and the SFC-authorised Gold Token (0.001 oz, no fees, bank margin up to 2%, bullion vaulted by HSBC Bank plc in London), plus custody and dealing for the Hang Seng Gold ETF. Singapore, Taiwan and Australia have no gold product at all; India only the HSBC AMC gold ETF; Malaysia a counter-only gold account (conventional bank, not Amanah); China gold-linked structured deposits. HSBC is not on India's 2026 to 2029 list of authorised bullion importers. Global Private Banking is overweight gold and reaches HK products through the local bank. src ● sourced, not re-checked
  • ANZ (Private >A$9bn): a liquidity competitor, not a shelf competitor. ANZ Private's only metals product is the State Street Gold Fund it seeded in July 2024 (feeder into SPDR Gold MiniShares, 0.14%, A$128m by Aug 2026, mostly price appreciation), backed by a maintained overweight. No allocated metal, physical, Lombard or gold-linked notes; retail broking went to CMC, Asian wealth to DBS in 2018, the Singapore vault closed in 2019. ANZ is an LBMA full member, not a market maker, and its institutional commodities trading assets rose 42% to A$9.1bn in FY2025. src ● sourced, not re-checked
  • Standard Chartered: SCB's wealth gold shelf is thin relative to its bullion-desk footprint: the only explicit metal balance for wealth clients is an unallocated line in Jersey, there is no gold account in SG/HK/MY/KR/TW, no physical/allocated custody for PB clients (physical gold, options financing and the planned HK vault are CIB-only), Singapore PCI excludes XAU, and India has lost its import licence for 2026-29. 4 minRisks and the questions you will get

Short answers ready for the room.

  • US tax: physical gold and grantor-trust ETFs are taxed as collectibles at 28%; IRA metal must sit at an approved depository. Design the US product around that (allocated at a depository, IRA-eligible). checked ✓ verified
  • Conduct: unallocated accounts are a claim on the bank, not metal; say so in the T&Cs (BEA's Gold Account brochure and HSBC's Wayfoong Statement Gold key facts do). Buy-back policy is a promise that gets tested (OCBC Hong Kong withdrew bar buy-back in February 2026). src checked ◔ partly verified, text amended
  • Regulation: mainland China froze new paper-gold positions in 2022 and the retreat is accelerating; Shariah products need an immediate-ownership structure such as the bai' as-sarf contract Maybank's MIGA-i describes. checked ◔ partly verified, text amended
  • Operations: UOB moved its gold counters to appointment-only in February 2026 under demand; logistics and vault capacity are part of the product. src checked ✎ corrected in text
  • Conflicts: Citi is already fund-level custodian or note issuer for competitor products (Betashares QAU per its PDS, where JPMorgan holds the bullion; Citi gold-linked notes on the Wells Fargo shelf). Map them before launch. checked ◔ partly verified, text amended
  • Counterparty: in our dataset, 45 of the 91 largest competitor metals books name no liquidity provider. That is the desk's pitch list, and it is the same names the wealth business will compete with. ⚠ not independently supported
